About the Lab

Dedicated to research on and treatment for eating disorders.

The EAT Lab has a six-pronged mission, all centered on improving the lives of those with eating disorders: conducting cutting-edge research to develop novel treatments, providing high-quality evidence-based treatment, training the next generation of researchers and clinicians, distributing prevention programs across the community, and advocating for better care and access.

Located in the Department of Psychological & Brain Sciences at the University of Louisville, directed by Dr. Cheri A. Levinson.

Our Research

Three research aims guide our work.

We approach eating disorders as heterogeneous, personal, and closely tied to anxiety — and we build treatment that reflects that.

01

Novel treatment development

Exposure therapy is the treatment of choice for anxiety disorders. We're working on ways to adapt exposure and other empirically-based anxiety treatments for eating disorders, where they've been historically underused.

02

Personalized ED treatment

Current treatments work for only about 50% of people who receive them. Using network analysis, we identify how symptoms differ within individuals — informing treatment that matches the person, not just the diagnosis.

03

ED & anxiety comorbidity

More than 70% of people with eating disorders also meet criteria for an anxiety disorder. We study why these two disorders co-occur so frequently — with the goal of developing concurrent interventions.

Our Research & Publications

Our program of research spans novel treatment development, personalization through computational psychiatry, and the intersection of eating disorders and anxiety.

Approach

How we do the work.

We combine clinical trials, ecological momentary assessment, network analysis, virtual reality, and community partnership.

Network analysis

Dynamic network modeling to identify how a person's symptoms connect and reinforce each other over time, then use those maps to guide treatment.

Momentary assessment

Short daily surveys on participants' phones give us signal on how symptoms unfold in real life, not just in clinic visits.

Virtual reality exposure

We develop and test VR-based exposure tools that let clinicians simulate feared situations safely and systematically.
